Methodology & Glossary

Plain-language definitions of everything used in this tool, and how the numbers are actually built.

The Three Treaties

Quota ShareYou and the reinsurer split every single claim (and every premium rupee) in the same fixed percentage. Simple and predictable, but doesn't specifically target your biggest claims.
Excess of LossThe reinsurer pays the part of any individual claim above an agreed amount ("retention"), up to a limit. Specifically targets large, unusual claims.
Stop LossThe reinsurer pays if your TOTAL claims for the whole year go above an agreed amount. Doesn't care about individual claims — only protects against a bad year overall.

Key Numbers

VaR 99% (Value at Risk)The 99th percentile of the simulated annual loss distribution. In this tool it is used as a simplified 1-in-100-year proxy, not a literal prediction that a loss will occur exactly once every 100 years.
Risk removedHow much smaller the simulated VaR 99% becomes after applying a given treaty.
RetentionThe amount you cover yourself before the reinsurer starts paying.
LimitThe most the reinsurer will pay under that treaty.
ReinstatementIn this simplified XOL model, annual ceded capacity is limit × (1 + reinstatements). Actual reinstatement premiums and treaty wording can be more complex.
Technical premiumAn illustrative risk-based estimate: expected ceded loss + risk loading (k × standard deviation), followed by the selected expense & profit loading.

How the numbers are produced

We simulate 6,000 possible years of claims using your inputs (claim frequency, typical claim size, variability, and catastrophe exposure), then apply each treaty to that same set of simulated years so the comparison is fair. Using the same simulated loss experience means differences between treaties are driven by the treaty structure rather than different random samples. Stress scenarios keep treaty terms fixed and change only the loss environment.

Pricing & modelling assumptions

Quota Share commissionThe model assumes a fixed 30% ceding commission for illustration.
XOL / Stop Loss premiumIndicative technical premium = expected ceded loss + risk loading, followed by the selected expense & profit loading. It is not a market quotation.
Decision scoreA model-specific heuristic based on budget fit, risk fit and simulated risk reduction. It is not a regulatory or industry-standard capital metric.
FrequencyPoisson is the baseline claim-count model; Negative Binomial allows greater claim-count variability (overdispersion).
SeverityLognormal and Gamma model positive, right-skewed claim sizes; Pareto is calibrated to the selected mean and illustrates heavier tail risk.

Model Scope & Limitations

This is an educational stochastic reinsurance model for exploring treaty mechanics, indicative pricing, tail risk and stress testing.

Catastrophe modelCatastrophe events are represented using simplified annual occurrence probabilities and severity assumptions rather than a full catastrophe model.
Not modelledNo geographic accumulation, event footprints, claim development, inflation, exposure growth, investment income, tax, regulatory capital framework or explicit dependency structure.
Stress testingThe selected treaty terms remain fixed so the test measures resilience to worse losses rather than changes in the contract.
InterpretationResults are illustrative and should not be treated as a market quote, regulatory capital result or substitute for advice from a qualified actuary or reinsurance broker.
